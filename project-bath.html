Case Study · Atlanta, GA · Specified

Lisa & Alex's bath, fixed without moving a wall.

One hall bath, one weekend of demolition, and a shopping list. Nothing moved: same tub, same drain, same door. The room reads twice its size because the surfaces stopped competing.

Scope
Hall bath · finishes and fixtures only
Tier
Tier 2 · specified selections with backups
Status
Specified · install scheduled

Concept

Three materials, and nothing else.

White oak, aged brass, white tile. Every decision in the room answers to those three, which is what keeps a small budget from looking like a small budget.

The Move

Spend the money on the two surfaces you touch, and leave the plumbing alone.

Moving a drain in this house means opening a finished ceiling downstairs. The budget went to the vanity, the counter and the brass instead, and the tub stayed exactly where it was.

The framed newspaper stays, at the owners' insistence. It is the best thing in the room.

Before

A builder-grade bath, doing its best.

What was specified

Six lines, each with a backup.

  • Vanity — white oak, three drawers, 36 inch
  • Top — quartz, undermount basin, 4 inch backsplash
  • Fittings — aged brass, widespread faucet, matching ring and hook
  • Tile — 3x6 white gloss, warm grey grout, stacked to the ceiling
  • Floor — marble-look herringbone, existing subfloor
  • Lighting — three-globe brass bar, warm 2700K
